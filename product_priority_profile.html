Product segmentation

Different products need different supply chain priorities.

Products differ in demand predictability, margin, unit value, shelf life, replenishment time, and the consequences of a stockout. A single priority profile therefore rarely fits every product.

Service performance

Availability, responsiveness, reliability, and customer satisfaction.

Cost efficiency

Purchasing, production, transportation, and operating expenses.

Capital efficiency

Inventory, assets, capacity, and cash tied up in the supply chain.

    Key takeaway

    One priority profile does not fit every product.

    Your five profiles are compared below. Different demand patterns, margins, stockout consequences, and obsolescence risks create different priority signatures.

    Segment by product characteristics

    Demand, value, margin, lead time, stockout consequences, and obsolescence risk should shape the priority profile.

    Multiple priorities can be high

    A seasonal product may require strong service during the selling window and strong capital discipline to avoid leftover stock.

    Use profiles to guide later decisions

    The priority profile provides the basis for differentiated inventory, sourcing, production, and fulfilment choices.

    Effective product segmentation means assigning differentiated supply chain priorities—not treating every product as if it creates the same operational requirements.
