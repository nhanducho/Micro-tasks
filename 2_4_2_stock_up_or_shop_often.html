Key takeaway

The best balance depends on the product

The same logic applies to companies and households. Inventory decisions should reflect demand, perishability, storage, replenishment speed, and the cost of running out.

Stable, non-perishable products

Buying more can reduce cost and protect availability when storage is easy and demand is predictable.

Perishable products

Buying small quantities more often reduces waste and inventory, even when the unit cost is higher.

Essential products

A reasonable safety quantity protects service when replenishment is temporarily difficult.

Household-to-business connection: Companies face the same trade-off at a much larger scale. The goal is not to maximize service, minimize cost, or eliminate inventory independently. The goal is to choose a sensible balance for the product and situation.
Reading the triangle: Service means availability, Cost means purchase and replenishment efficiency, and Inventory means keeping money and storage space tied up in stock under control.
