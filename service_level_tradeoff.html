Inventory strategy

More inventory can improve service—but it also creates cost.

Companies must decide how much inventory to hold. More inventory usually makes products more available, but it also ties up money and may create obsolete stock.

Key insight

The highest service level is not always the best choice.

The appropriate position depends on what the company sells and what matters most to its customers.

⚠️

Stockout consequences

When a stockout could cause serious harm or disruption, higher inventory and service are usually justified.

⏳

Obsolescence risk

When products lose value quickly, companies need to balance availability against excess and outdated stock.

💰

Cost pressure

When low price is the main advantage, tighter inventory can reduce cost even if occasional stockouts occur.

Remember: inventory and service decisions should support the company’s business strategy—not simply maximize availability.
