One product launch, three competing priorities

A successful launch is not only about selling the product. The supply chain must protect customer service, control cost, and avoid building too much inventory too early.

Key takeaway

Good launches are balanced, not one-sided

If one department dominates the decision, the launch may be strong in one area but weak in another. Supply-chain management is about balancing the trade-off.

Service wins customers

Strong availability helps the launch succeed and protects the customer experience.

Cost protects efficiency

Good purchasing, transport, and production choices matter, especially when volumes rise.

Inventory ties up money

Too much stock creates risk and locks resources into products that may not sell as expected.

Practical lesson: In a launch, the best answer is rarely “maximize one corner.” A better answer is to create enough availability, keep options open, and avoid overcommitting too early.
This task used inventory as the third corner because inventory is one of the clearest forms of working capital in a launch decision.
You may also see the same idea described as a service–cost–capital trade-off. In many real cases, inventory is a key way capital gets tied up.
